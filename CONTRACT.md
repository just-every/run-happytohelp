# HappyToHelp product contract

Target edition · 17 October 2026 · US-first, USD. HappyToHelp is a complete customer service help desk and AI support agent for a US SMB support lead switching from Zendesk, Intercom (with Fin), Help Scout or Freshdesk. It owns the job from customer intake through a shared operational queue, self-service and live business context to an authorized resolution and continued customer recovery. Decagon, Sierra and Pylon set the agent/procedure and B2B-channel baseline; the target goes further with verified repair and obligations that survive handoffs.

This is the product we are building towards. Required behavior is not a claim of current implementation, provider acceptance or compliance. The final table records current code/test coverage as done, partial or missing; each clause names the public journey that will prove it. The matching evidence map is [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md). A cited test is implementation evidence, not a claim that it was executed for this document change.

Keep the owner-approved homepage, UI and behavior intact. The retained owner decisions of 8 October (native passwords), 9 October (import equality and bounded ordinary HTTPS transport) and the detailed PUBLIC-001 specification remain in force. This contract expansion authorizes future build rounds against the missing work; it does not silently change today's marketing claims. The product remains editable source on Cloudflare Workers with a separately authorized local connector, run through `rer`.

## Retained product and operating guarantees

  Reset is explicitly confirmed and local;
  export/import preserve supported native data with documented exclusions.
  Deployment dry-run makes zero outbound requests and explicitly reports that fact. A separately and explicitly started read-only provider preflight uses the owner's scoped Cloudflare token with owner consent to read the account's Workers subdomain and the Worker's settings and secret names (three GETs for an existing Worker); it sends no mail and changes no resources. Dry-run and preflight are independent steps, neither a mock of the other. The delivered runtime requires no original checkout or PHP service.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Use a clean checkout through rer describe/check, locked installation, setup, dev, verify, reset and export/import; preserve owner/data/settings and refuse unconfirmed destructive work. For deployment dry-run, assert zero outbound requests and an explicit no-outbound report without provider credentials. As a separate acceptance point, explicitly start the read-only provider preflight with owner consent and the owner's scoped token; assert only the three expected Cloudflare GETs for an existing Worker, no mail and no mutations, and refuse missing credentials or consent. Prove the runtime works independently of the original checkout; separately qualify remote provisioning.

## Target clauses

### Complete support operations

- **DESK-001 — The shared support desk.** A visitor starts website live chat, identifies themselves under the workspace policy, sends files and returns to their history. Humans see one durable shared inbox with customer history, scoped retained-history search with words, quoted phrases and literal customer-identity matching, open/closed/mine/unassigned/AI/tag filters and saved replies that insert into the composer without silently sending. Ticket status follows the STATUS-001 lifecycle and saved replies extend to MACRO-001 action macros. Replies, attachment downloads and errors are visible on both sides; privacy/collector controls remain effective.

  **Acceptance evidence:** Through the installed widget and operator inbox, exchange messages and real attachment bytes, search/filter the resulting queue, insert and edit a saved reply, reload both sides and deny another tenant the same records.

- **WIDGET-001 — Availability-aware messenger and proactive messages.** The messenger shows team availability from business-hour schedules (SLA-001) and live agent presence. When no human or permitted AI assistant is available it says so with the expected reply time, captures the visitor's email (or, where consented, SMS/WhatsApp under CHANNELS-001) so the answer reaches them, and creates the ticket instead of leaving the visitor waiting. Leads configure proactive messages triggered by page URL, time on page, scroll depth, exit intent, visitor attributes, returning visit or cart value, with audience, brand, frequency caps and quiet hours. Each proactive message shows a clear sender identity and an easy dismiss, respects consent and collector settings, and never starts a conversation that sends personal data on the visitor's behalf. Impressions, opens, dismissals and resulting conversations and resolutions are reported. This clause backs the homepage hero's "Before they ask".

  **Acceptance evidence:** Outside configured hours, open the installed widget, see the offline message and expected reply time, leave an email and verify the ticket and the emailed answer. During hours with no agent online verify the same; with an available AI assistant verify it answers and discloses itself. Configure a proactive message on a checkout URL after 20 seconds with a once-per-day cap; verify it appears once, dismisses, stays silent under a declined collector setting and appears in reports with its impressions and conversations.

- **MAIL-001 — Complete support email.** Connect inbound forwarding, an IMAP/SMTP mailbox, Gmail/Google Workspace or Microsoft 365/Outlook using scoped credentials/OAuth. New mail creates tickets and replies thread by stable Message-ID/In-Reply-To/References with deliberate handling of changed subject, aliases and multiple recipients. Operators manage CC/BCC, attachments, signatures and branded sender/reply addresses. Duplicate/redelivered mail, bounces, spam and auto-responders cannot create loops or send twice, and mail classified as spam or failing sender checks goes to the reviewable Suspended view of MAIL-002; reconnect/backfill has a visible cursor and errors, and removal stops new intake without deleting history.

  **Acceptance evidence:** For each of forwarding, IMAP, Gmail and Microsoft 365, use the connection UI and real provider test accounts to receive a new thread, reply from the inbox, receive its follow-up and attachment, disconnect/reconnect with redelivery and observe bounce/permission failure. Verify no duplicate ticket/reply or machine-mail AI loop; keep provider-protocol tests separate from live acceptance. Spam and failing-sender mail reaches the MAIL-002 Suspended view, not a ticket.

- **MAIL-002 — Reviewable suspended and spam mail.** Inbound mail that is spam-scored, fails DMARC/SPF/DKIM or alignment, comes from a blocked sender or domain, forges a verified customer's address, exceeds size limits or is a detected mail loop is never silently dropped. It lands in a Suspended view with its reason, authentication results, headers and safely previewed attachments, and creates no ticket, AI work, acknowledgment or notification. Permitted operators recover a false positive into a normal ticket that threads correctly (into the existing ticket when its references match) without retroactively triggering acknowledgments or AI unless they choose to; delete true spam; and block or allow a sender or domain. Recovery, deletion and list changes are audited. Suspended mail is kept for a stated retention period (default 14 days) and then explicitly deleted with an audit record; the view shows counts and the oldest age so nothing ages out unseen. - **API-002 — Create and append support requests from another product or agent.** A workspace owner/admin issues a named machine credential for one source product or agent (for example a handoff from Handset) and binds it to exactly what it may touch; the scoped workspace API then lets that product create a ticket and append to it without a human session. Credentials are extensions of the CONNECTIONS-001 token model, not a second system: bearer-only, no cookies, no CORS, secret shown once at issue and stored only as a hash, with a non-secret display prefix, name, source-product label and the issuing member, creation time, optional expiry (UTC), last-used time and last-used source visible in Settings. Issue, rotate and revoke require the current owner/admin with step-up; rotation issues a new secret and keeps the old one valid for an owner-chosen overlap (default none, maximum 7 days) before it retires, and revocation, expiry, a blocked or deleted workspace and a restore take effect on the next request, with an in-flight create either committed whole or refused with nothing stored. Scopes are separate and narrow: `conversations:create`, `conversations:read` (bound by default to the conversations the credential created), `messages:append` (customer-side message or internal note on a conversation inside its bounds) and, unchanged, the existing operator-style `messages:write`; a read-only, a create-only and an append-only credential can each be issued, and no scope implies another. Bindings limit the credential to named brands, mailboxes/sender addresses, groups, request forms and an API channel label (`api:<source product>`, a first-class channel in inbox filters, reports, SLA and triage); it may set only priority levels and existing workspace tags its bindings allow (an unknown tag is refused, never created), may not assign, choose status beyond New, write internal-only fields or act as an operator, and an operation outside its bounds returns 404 for another resource and 403 `outside_credential_bounds` for a disallowed value, never a silent adjustment. Create takes a requester identified by email, E.164 phone or the caller's own external ID (namespaced to its source product) with optional name; subject, plain-text body and attachments passed by reference (a prior bounded upload id or a public HTTPS URL fetched under the existing egress rules with a required SHA-256, no redirects, size and 10-second bounds and the widget's type rules, the whole request atomic so one bad attachment stores no ticket); source channel label, priority and tags; and a source-product reference `{product, ref, url}` where the product label is fixed by the credential and cannot be spoofed, the URL is HTTPS without embedded credentials and is shown to operators as a back-link ("Created via Handset, open in Handset") and never fetched, and a second create for the same source reference under a different key returns 409 `source_ref_exists` with the existing ticket ID so the caller appends instead. The `Idempotency-Key` header is required on create (1-200 characters, scoped to workspace and source product so a rotated or reissued credential still deduplicates, retained at least 30 days): the same key and canonical body return the original status and body marked as a replay, a different body returns 409 `idempotency_conflict`, and a key still in flight returns 409 `in_progress` with `Retry-After`; append is idempotent by the existing `localId`. The identity rule is CUSTOMER-001's: a requester matches an existing contact only by an exact verified key, a new contact created by the credential is marked as asserted by that credential and unverified, email or name text alone never grants history access or merges contacts, two identifiers that point at different contacts return 409 `identity_conflict` without disclosing either contact, a requester matching an erasure tombstone is refused with `identity_erased`, and responses have the same shape whether or not the contact already existed. Append posts a message to an existing conversation as the requester (kind `customer`) or as an internal note (kind `note`), never as an operator or as AI; a `customer` message reopens a Solved ticket and creates the linked follow-up of a Closed one under STATUS-001, a merged ticket answers 409 `merged_into` with the surviving ID, and attachments follow the same reference rule. API-created tickets are untrusted customer content: they pass the same triage, automations, routing and AI-agent rules as any new ticket with source `api`, and body text, subject and source fields can never instruct the agent or open an external effect. They respect spam, abuse and consent: blocked senders, blocked identities and per-requester create caps (default 5 per hour, owner-configurable) place the request in Suspended under MAIL-002 with a reason and a 202 `held_for_review` response and no ticket-created event, AI request or outbound message; acknowledgments and any customer-facing send follow NOTIFY-001 and CHANNELS-001, an email acknowledgment is sent only when the caller supplies a consent record (channel, basis, time) the workspace accepts for that source, an SMS or WhatsApp send needs recorded opt-in and an approved template and an opted-out number is refused while the ticket is still created, and a created ticket otherwise contacts nobody. Signed webhooks use the existing `integrations.webhook.v1` signing, delivery and retry policy and add `conversation.status_changed` (ticket ID, previous and new status category and ID, source reference) and `conversation.assigned`; a destination can be limited to tickets created by one credential or one source product, and events keep carrying identifiers and values, never transcript text, email addresses or phone numbers. Limits are explicit: the existing per-credential requests-per-minute limit plus a separate create quota per minute and per day configured on the credential under workspace and installation ceilings, and the per-requester cap, each refused with 429, `Retry-After`, `RateLimit-Limit/Remaining/Reset` headers and a machine-readable code naming which limit (`credential`, `requester` or `workspace`); oversize bodies return 413 `payload_too_large` and unsupported types 415, nothing is silently truncated or dropped, and storage or plan exhaustion returns 503 `unavailable` with `Retry-After` and no partial ticket. Errors use the existing `{error:{code,message}}` shape with stable codes. Every action is audited under AUDIT-001 with a machine actor named on the credential: issue, rotation, scope or binding change, first use, expiry, revocation and denied attempts (counted per credential and window, not one event per probe), and every create or append records the credential name, source product and reference, request ID, idempotency-key digest and outcome in ticket history ("Created by API credential Handset support handoff"), never presented to the customer as a person, and the credential's name survives its revocation while an erased issuer is redacted without losing the event. Erasure under ERASURE-001 removes personal content from API-created tickets, stored replay bodies, pending event payloads and attachment bytes, keeps an opaque tombstone and the source reference without personal data, and fences a stale replay or a later create from resurrecting the identity. Portability under PORTABILITY-001 exports API-created tickets with channel label, source reference and machine-actor name, and exports credential metadata (ID, name, source product, scopes, bindings, creation, expiry, revocation, last-used) without hash or secret; restore creates every credential revoked and inert so the receiving owner must reissue, carries idempotency records as key and body digests only, and replays nothing. Documentation (`docs/api.md`) lists the new operations, scopes, bindings and error codes with runnable examples that keep tokens out of command arguments.
